## How I judged

- **Supported**: the cited rows show what the claim says, on the right platform and sector, Rwandan where it says Rwandan, with a matching number. At least 2 matching rows.
- **Partly**: some of the claim is shown and some is not (a number, a platform, a sector, a country, a "long-running" or "most-liked" comparison), or only one row matches, or some cited rows do not fit.
- **Not supported**: the cited rows contradict the claim or do not show it at all.
- Where a claim quotes a count (22 of 51, 94 of 450, 7 of 20, 44 of 177, 11 of 20) I recomputed it from the jsonl files. I did not recompute the Instagram "13 of 34 Kigali reels" or the TikTok "11 of 17 Rwandan ads" (a keyword check on Instagram found 10 of 36; the other is a read-out of watched rows).
- Rows are judged by what the row says, using the hook, on-screen text and caption fields. Many rows were watched muted for about 7 to 12 s, so "ends on" or "held for" claims are weaker than "opens with" claims.
- No scam row (income chains, MoMo-screenshot posts, herbal remedies) is cited as positive evidence; they appear only in `no-fake-income` and `no-income-promise` as the examples to avoid. The Chinese campus clip (@village988) appears only in `look-real`, as the example of an AI-looking clip posted as a Rwandan school.

## Problems that need a fix first

1. **`price-first-frame`: the reason text is wrong.** It says "the long-running East African Facebook ads all show the offer at second 0". Four of five cited rows are 5 to 26 days old and only Fitae (522 d) is long-running. The long-runner table in `facebook-patterns.md` says only 7 of 20 long runners show any price. The rule itself (price in the first frame) is fine for short-lived ads; change the reason.
2. **`no-vague-discount` is not supported.** Its only row (tt-097, "Drop 40% or 60% now") is the most-liked phone-shop clip in the set (67.8K likes), and it is a **Ghanaian** store (#GhanaBusiness, a Ghana number), not Rwandan. `tiktok-patterns.md` calls it "the most-liked Rwandan phone-shop video"; that is a mistake in the pattern file. It also weakens `native-look` (compares a Ghanaian shop to two AI creators) and counts in `real-place`, `direct-question`.
3. **`momo` is out of date.** "None of the 51 Rwandan Facebook video ads mentions MoMo" is true for those 51, but the Sofar Kigali ticket ad (Ad Library id 981129234484465, round 4) says "MoMo: 0788617015 (Ornella)" in its caption. The research summary and `captions-patterns.md` repeat "none"; they should say "rare" or "none of the first 51 and 111 captions".
4. **`one-clip-two-platforms` counts one ad too many.** TikTok watermarks appear in two cited Facebook ads (Eguriro, SPOT SALON); the third row (yt-020) is a YouTube Short.
5. **`budget-brand-look` overclaims.** "Every small Rwandan seller shows the number" fails for petal_florist_kigali (a 3 s loop with no number in the video, running 228 days), and Piramie (cited as having "no number") shows a price pill.
6. **`hands-if-no-person`'s reason** says hands carry "the long-running local ads"; the cited Facebook row is 26 days old and the other three are TikTok.
7. **`first-caption-0s`**: the "about 5 words, upper-middle" part is not measured in any row.
8. **Country labels in `evidence.json`.** fb-026 (Modesees) is labelled RW because the ad targets Rwanda; it is a global AI-model ad, not a Rwandan business. Several rows have country `?` (tt-097 is Ghana). Rules that say "Rwandan" (`language-mix`, `price-first-frame`, `landmark`) also cite Tanzanian, Indian and Algerian rows; the Rwandan rows alone still support them except where noted.

Main findings:

2. **Three TikTok formulas lean on non-Rwandan rows**: `price-list-caption` (a "090" Nigerian number), `question-then-offer` (Ghana; its "Drop 40% or 60%" is the vague discount the Brain forbids) and `book-service` (South Africa).

4. **Two examples are paraphrases, not captions**: the Nairobi line in `ig-owner-contact` (the real Brimat caption says "Call/WhatsApp: 0711 179 871 ... Tom Mboya Street ...") and the "branches" example.
