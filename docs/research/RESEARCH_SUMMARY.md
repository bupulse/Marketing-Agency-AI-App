# What we learned from 600+ real videos: a summary for the owner

Written 17 October 2026 from the five pattern files in `docs/research/reels/` (Facebook, TikTok, Instagram, YouTube Shorts and captions). It is for a business owner, not a developer. Every number says where it came from and how many videos it is based on. The honest limits are in section 6, so read them before you trust a number.

**What was studied**

| Source | Rows | What they are |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Facebook | 159 | Meta Ad Library video ads (mostly Rwandan), plus 20 organic reels from 12 official Rwandan business pages |
| TikTok | 179 + 450 comments | 133 videos watched in the owner's TikTok, 46 read about in articles, and the first comments under 30 clips |
| Instagram | 101 | 58 global reels and 43 reels from 36 East African accounts (34 of them Kigali) |
| YouTube Shorts | 36 | Logged-out Shorts from nine searches |
| Captions | 125 | The words under Rwandan business posts (111 Rwandan) |

Everything was watched with the sound off, for about 7 to 12 seconds per video. Nothing was liked, followed, commented on or downloaded.

---

## 1. The 20 findings that matter most

1. **People ask "how much?" more than anything else.** 94 of 450 comments (21 percent) under 30 TikTok clips ask the price, and 69 of those are on clips that show no price. Source: TikTok comments, 30 clips.
2. **The price must be on screen from the first second, with its condition.** 11 of 17 Rwandan TikTok shop, food and phone ads show a price, offer or number in the first 3 seconds (TikTok, 17 ads). In Facebook ads the price strip is the usual first frame. A price with a hidden condition makes people doubt it: "28k only" drew "is the price real?" (TikTok comments).
3. **A phone or WhatsApp number is almost always in the ad.** It is in the caption of 63 percent of Rwandan captions (111) and the "Send WhatsApp message" button is used on 22 of 51 Rwandan Facebook ads. On Instagram, 13 of 34 Kigali reels have the number on screen the whole time (Instagram, 34 reels).
4. **Reels with a number on screen got more plays.** Median 58.8K plays against 39.7K without it (Instagram Kigali, 34 reels, top reels only, so it points the right way but is not proof).
5. **People still ask where.** 10 percent of comments ask the location (TikTok, 450 comments). Rwandan ads and posts that do it well name a landmark: "Next to Delight Hotel", "opposite IPRC traffic lights", "Makuza Peace Plaza, Room 35" (Facebook and captions).
6. **Most questions get no visible answer.** Only 44 of 177 question comments (25 percent) had a seller reply beside them. Replies that said the price or the address were the most liked (TikTok comments).
7. **Length differs by platform.** Median 34 s for Rwandan Facebook ads (51 ads), 27 s for Kigali Instagram reels (34), 15 s for YouTube Shorts (36), 20 s for organic Facebook reels (20), 15 to 25 s for Rwandan TikTok ads (17).
8. **Phone-filmed, real shop, real person beats polished.** The best Rwandan shop, salon and phone-shop clips are handheld or one locked shot of the real counter, the real treatment, the real seller (TikTok and Instagram, about 50 clips). The glossy AI product clips had 3 to 7K likes (TikTok).
9. **Small accounts can go far.** Median plays on Kigali Instagram reels are about 21 times the follower count. A 59-follower car-hire page reached 110K plays. People watch and then message: likes are only about 0.55 percent of plays, so measure messages, not likes (Instagram, 34 reels).
10. **Services lead with trust, shops lead with price.** Clinics and schools open with a doctor, an insurer list, "since 1969" or a date; salons, fashion and car hire open with a price (TikTok, 40 thin-sector clips).
11. **One format, many variants, wins.** One clinic ran 17 variants of the same "doctor, one condition, sentence captions" video since March. An internet provider ran 6 in 11 days. (Facebook Ad Library.)
12. **Long-running ads are long and rarely show a price.** Of 20 non-scam ads live for 97 to 565 days: median 48 s, only 7 show a price, 11 have a number in the caption, 17 are in English (Facebook, 20 ads).
13. **Weekly offers work as a series.** "Buy 1 get 1 free every Tuesday", "Friday Unlocked: buy 4, get 5", "every Friday promo" repeat the same badge weekly (TikTok restaurant ads, Facebook event and food ads).
14. **Language is mostly English with Kinyarwanda touches.** Of 111 Rwandan captions: 77 English, 19 mixed, 14 Kinyarwanda, 1 French (captions). Kinyarwanda dominates for plots, houses, cheap goods and baby products; English for hotels, clinics, tech and beauty. About 27 percent of TikTok comments are in Kinyarwanda.
15. **Hashtags: few on Facebook, more on TikTok.** Facebook ad captions: median 1, 49 percent none. TikTok: median 5. Organic Facebook reels: none on 14 of 20. Recurring tags: #Kigali (17 of 125 captions), #Rwanda (14), #RwandaBusiness (5). #MadeInRwanda did not appear.
16. **Write phone numbers the way people write them.** Of 120 numbers: 78 local 10-digit with no spaces (0788218567), 22 as "+250 788 123 456", 10 "+250788123456". The usual label is "Call/WhatsApp:". Kinyarwanda calls to action: "Duhamagare", "Twandikire kuri WhatsApp", "Ntucikwe" (captions).
17. **The message must work with sound off.** Of 30 TikTok clips, 12 carried the message in text alone and 11 in picture alone; only 7 depended on a voice. YouTube Shorts: no Short used "subscribe"; the platform draws its own button and title, so keep text in the middle of the frame (TikTok 30 clips; YouTube 36).
18. **Organic posts are not ads.** The reels that got reach were brand moments, not offers (see section 4): a silent hotel film got 310K views, national football highlights 54K, a buy-one-get-one burger reel 163 (Facebook, 20 organic reels).
19. **People ask "is it real?"** Two TikTok clips drew "is this AI?", one a school that was not Rwandan. A real shop, a real person and the real product answer most doubts (TikTok comments, 450).
20. **Long-running does not always mean good.** About half of the 69 Rwandan ads that started before 15 August are income-chain and recruiter ads. They run long because they are cheap and re-posted, not because they are honest (Facebook Ad Library, 69 ads).

## 2. What a good Kigali video looks like, platform by platform

### Facebook ad (Reels and feed)
| | |
| --- | --- |
| Length | 15 to 30 s for goods; 45 to 90 s only with a person talking (clinic, property, school). A 3 to 6 s loop is fine for flowers, shoes, skincare. |
| First 3 seconds | The object or the label already on screen: a price strip, a "NEW" badge, a location stack for property, a question. Product working, not a logo. |
| Price | Big, top third, held all the way: "48,000 RWF", "from 20,000 RWF". For services, "book a consultation" or "free quote" can replace a price. |
| Number | A phone bar from the first to the last frame, plus the button "Send WhatsApp message". Two numbers are fine. |
| Language | English with Kinyarwanda for plots, goods, baby products; English for hotels, clinics, tech. Product names and RWF stay as they are. |
| Text | One phrase per beat or one fixed strip. Never more than two lines at once. |
| Sound | Not needed to understand the video. Add music underneath. |
| Caption | Price in RWF, area with a landmark, delivery line, the phone twice, one call to action. Property and clinic captions use one fact per line with an icon. |
| Hashtags | 0 to 3. |

### TikTok
| | |
| --- | --- |
| Length | 15 to 25 s, a change of picture or text every 2 to 4 s. |
| First 3 seconds | A question or "POV: you just found the most affordable [product] in Kigali", with the price or offer on screen. A real person and a real shelf. |
| Price | A solid price card that stays up, with its condition ("28k with extension"). |
| Number | Held to the end, in the middle of the frame, not in the bottom 15 percent (the app covers it). |
| Language | Kinyarwanda hook, English product and price, bilingual call to action ("Karibu", "duhamagare cg twandikire"). |
| Text | Large, centred, word by word; always burned in. |
| Sound | Message first in text; use the platform's own music for the mood: Afrobeats-style for salon, fashion, travel; quiet for clinics and schools. |
| Caption | Offer first, then how to order, in the first line. |
| Hashtags | 4 to 6: #Kigali or #KigaliTikTok, #RwandaBusiness, one sector tag, one brand tag. |

### Instagram reels
| | |
| --- | --- |
| Length | 15 to 30 s for Kigali services and shop walk-throughs (median 27 s); 6 to 15 s for a one-idea product clip. |
| First 3 seconds | Text at frame 0, 3 to 7 words: the claim, the price or the question. For a service, the finished result or the treatment already happening. |
| Price | On screen for the whole clip if the product has one; also in the caption. |
| Number | On screen from frame 0, in a bar at the top or lower third. Required for Kigali. |
| Language | English first, Kinyarwanda line for local trust. |
| Text | Keep out of the bottom 20 percent and the right 15 percent. One caption style per video. |
| Sound | Often original or a licensed track; do not rely on it. |
| Caption | The sales page: price in RWF, sizes, "free delivery in Kigali", landmark, MoMo or "payment after delivery", a comment keyword ("Comment PRICE"). |
| Hashtags | 3 to 6 (two broad, two local, one or two niche). |

### YouTube Shorts
| | |
| --- | --- |
| Length | 10 to 15 s. One idea, then stop. Longer only for a list. |
| First 3 seconds | The best visual already in motion (macro of the product, the splash, the pour). A title with the promise or a number: "5 reasons...", "Day 12". |
| Price | In the middle of the frame, not the bottom (the channel name, title and Subscribe button cover it). |
| Number | A short pointer at the end: a phone number, a QR code, the shop name to search. No "subscribe"; YouTube draws it. |
| Language | English title; local language text inside the video. |
| Text | Big centred words, one highlight colour; leave the top 10 percent clear for YouTube's own captions. |
| Sound | Music under; still readable muted. |
| Hashtags | 1 to 3 at the end of the title. |
| Note | Craft wins here: 3D, motion graphics and macro product clips got 5K to 32K likes, plain shop footage got 4 to 2.9K. Local ads are rare on Shorts. |

## 3. What to put in the words under the video

From 125 Rwandan captions. Start with what is sold and the price in the first 60 characters. Then the delivery promise ("Free delivery in Kigali", "Payment after delivery"), the place with a landmark, one call to action in the owner's language, and the number written the usual way. Facebook captions are longer than TikTok ones (median 316 characters against 184). Five model captions are in `captions-patterns.md` (shop, restaurant, real estate, salon, school).

## 4. Ads against organic posts

Source: 20 organic Facebook reels from 12 official pages (RwandAir, Simba, Inyange, Skol, Bourbon Coffee, CANAL+, Bralirwa, ONOMO, Piramie, Inzovu, Abby Home, Java House) against about 50 Rwandan ads.

| | Organic post | Ad |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Length | median 20 s | median 34 s |
| Opening | a face, a place, a pour | a price strip or a question |
| Price on screen | 1 of 20 | most |
| Phone number | 1 of 20 | about two thirds of captions |
| Hashtags | 0 on 14 of 20 | median 5 when used |
| Sound | native or original on 16, licensed song on 4 | not known (watched muted) |
| Caption | median 13 words, no contact | a spec list with price and number |
| What gets reach | moments: staff greetings, event recaps, a hotel film (310K views), national football (54K) | a clear offer with a number, repeated in variants |

So the engine needs two modes. **Post mode**: no price strip or phone bar by default, a person or a moment first, 12 to 25 s, a short warm caption. **Ad mode**: price and number from the first frame, button, landmark. A post with an offer but no reach (a buy-one-get-one burger, 163 views) shows an offer alone is not enough.

## 5. Scam and trick patterns to never copy

1. **Income-chain posts** ("Tajyira Akakanya Gukorere Akayabo Kama Faranga..." and copies) and "work and earn 30K a day" recruiters. About 30 ads from 12 or more pages, running since June. They are among the longest-running Rwandan ads.
2. **Income proof with a screenshot**: a person in front of a Mobile Money SMS showing 1,200,000 RWF, a giant WhatsApp number on top (YouTube, TikTok).
3. **Herbal "men's health" or miracle remedies** with a "Hamagara Muganga" strip, and any cure claim.
4. **"Business wakora uyu munsi ugasezera k'ubukene"**, affiliate and make-money-online pages, casino bonus ads.
5. **A price that is not the real price**: "8k" on screen and "18k" in the reply; "28k" that needs extra. Always show the condition.
6. **Invented places or people**: a campus or shop that is not Rwandan, an AI-made seller or customer, a fake review.
7. **A fake "limited stock" or "discount"** the owner did not set. The engine must never invent a price, a stock level, a discount, a number or an address.
8. **Foreign exporter ads** that name Rwanda only to get reach.

## 6. Honest limits of this research

- **Muted, short viewing.** Every video was watched without sound for about 7 to 12 seconds from 3 to 5 frames. Voice, music, the ending and the pacing past that are estimates. Cuts per 10 seconds are rough.
- **Top-reel bias.** Many rows are the most viewed reels of a page or search (Instagram accounts, organic Facebook pages). They show what wins, not what is average, and survivors do not show what fails.
- **Ads: no results.** The Facebook Ad Library shows no views, likes or spend. "Works" means "has run a long time" or "the advertiser runs many variants". Days running count from the first start. Scam ads also run long.
- **Thin sectors.** Clinics are 5 Facebook rows and no pharmacy ad exists in the library; events, car hire and transport are 11 to 16 rows. Instagram East Africa is 43 rows. TikTok Rwandan business ads are 17 in the first batch.
- **Small Kinyarwanda samples.** 14 Rwandan captions are mostly Kinyarwanda; the estimate of how much Kinyarwanda a caption has is a rough lower bound from a word list. About 450 comments is a small base.
- **YouTube was logged out** and gave only likes and comments, no views or dates.
- **One snapshot.** Everything is from 17 October 2026. The Ad Library also changes by the hour.
- **Some cells are empty.** The like, comment and share counts are blank on most Ad Library rows; reaction numbers on reels are as read from the page.
- **Browser trouble.** The research Chrome was killed by mistake once and crashed tabs several times; some rows were rewatched later.

## 7. Open questions for a second round

1. Does a price on screen actually lead to more WhatsApp messages? We only see correlation. A test of the same video with and without the price strip would answer it.
2. What do Rwandans actually hear? A round watched with sound on, for music, voice and the language people speak in videos.
3. What works in Kinyarwanda? A larger, deliberate sample of Kinyarwanda captions and voice-overs, and whether Kinyarwanda hooks beat English ones.
4. Is MoMo mentioned anywhere, and does it help? None of the 111 Rwandan captions mentions it.
5. What does a typical (not top) Kigali business post look like and perform like? Pick pages at random, not by plays.
6. What do clinics, pharmacies, schools and events need? More rows, and what trust cues (insurers, licences, dates) move people.
7. Which sectors need what length? Does a 60 to 90 s talking video really beat 20 s for clinics and property?
8. How much does reach depend on the page's history? Small accounts did well on Instagram; is it the format or the algorithm?
9. Do people mind AI visuals in a Rwandan shop video? Test labelled against real footage.
10. What does the owner's own audience want? Real tests on the owner's pages beat any research of other people's videos.
